relay off
A page may not open a raw TCP socket, so the camera's RTSP can never be played here directly. What works: press this and the camera starts a small relay (go2rtc on the card) that repackages the same H.264 into fragmented MP4 over HTTP, and your machine decodes it — nothing is transcoded on the camera's 432 MHz core. The relay is not running at boot: it comes up on the press and shuts itself down a few minutes after the last viewer stops asking.

Image mode (ISP)
image mode: reading…
Night is the mode that measures neutral under this camera's own infrared light (R=G=B across 0..255); the same scene through a day-mode ISP measures R-G +76, B-G +79 — that is the purple picture. The change is applied while the stream keeps running, and it is remembered across restarts. Auto does not read the light sensor (measured: it reports DAY with the room light on and with it off). It reads the analogue gain the ISP's auto-exposure loop spends to hold its luminance target — about 4.5k when the room is lit, 12.5k in day mode and pegged at 16384 in night mode when it is dark. Day means lamp out and IR-cut filter in; night means lamp on and filter out. Before a night to day switch it puts the illuminator out for about 16 s and re-reads, so the decision is not made on the camera's own light; the picture goes dark for those seconds while it checks.
Two different things. The illuminator is a gpio and switches instantly (measured: mean luminance 142 → 127 with it off, automatic gain taking some of that back). The filter is mechanical and takes a couple of seconds to travel; with the illuminator on it measured mean 44 against 136, so leaving the room lit only by IR and putting the filter in will look like the picture went blind.
